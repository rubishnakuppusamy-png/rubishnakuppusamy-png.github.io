Week 4: Embedded Systems, Sensors, ESP32 and IoT

Forge Innovation & Ventures ~4 Min Read Rubishna Kuppusamy

Week 4 of ProtoSem focused on understanding how hardware and software seamlessly work together. We explored embedded systems, Arduino, sensors, ESP32, and the fundamentals of the Internet of Things (IoT). Through practical activities and mini-projects, we learned how to build, program, and control connected electronic systems.

Step 01

Getting Started with Arduino

We began by learning the architectural core of embedded systems and how microcontrollers interact with electronic components. We explored the Arduino IDE, discovering how to write, compile, and flash firmware onto physical development boards.

Hands-on Activity: Programmed digital I/O pins to cycle LEDs ON, OFF, and blink at timed intervals—solidifying how software instructions directly dictate physical state transitions.

ESP32 GPIO 2 LED Circuit Diagram

Physical LED Blink Test on Hardware

Step 02

Exploring Sensors and Actuators

We examined the dual feedback loop of smart systems: sensors collect environmental signals, while actuators execute physical actions driven by the microcontroller's logic.

Sensors (Inputs)

Capture analog and digital environmental variables (temperature, light, proximity).

Actuators (Outputs)

Trigger actions such as motors, buzzers, relays, and optical displays.

Step 03

Working with Arduino Sensors

We connected sensors to Arduino pinouts, processed signals with libraries, and logged live readings in the serial monitor:

Temperature & Humidity Sensor (DHT)

Learned how to detect environmental changes in real-time, parsing calibrated temperature and relative humidity telemetry.

Infrared (IR) Sensor

Explored infrared emitter/receiver pairs for proximity and obstacle detection, communicating digital high/low flags to the board.

DHT11 & Dual Alert LED Wiring Diagram

Live Hardware Interfacing with DHT11 & LED

Step 04

Exploring ESP32 & Interactive Projects

We leveled up to the dual-core ESP32 microcontroller, exploring its advanced pin matrix, processing power, and onboard Wi-Fi / Bluetooth capabilities. We applied these skills in two hands-on projects:

Ultrasonic Range Finder

Built a distance-measuring setup calculating sound wave travel time between trigger pulses and echo returns.

Joystick-Based Game

Engineered an interactive gaming experience combining dual-axis analog joystick inputs, program state machines, and ESP32 responsiveness.

Step 05

Introduction to IoT & Cloud Control

We unpacked the fundamental layers of the Internet of Things (IoT)—perception, network, and application layers—understanding how edge nodes exchange telemetry and receive remote commands over IP networks.

Wi-Fi Controlled Smart LED Circuit

Hooked an LED circuit up to wireless Wi-Fi and interfaced with it digitally. We turned the lighting ON/OFF and dynamically switched color modes across the network using web commands.

ESP32 IoT Telemetry & Status Dashboard

Web & Extension Interface for LED Control

Key Technical Takeaways

Embedded Systems, Microprocessors & Microcontrollers
Arduino IDE Firmware Toolchain & Flashing
Sensors, Actuators & Real-Time Data Collection
Hands-on DHT Temp/Humidity, IR & Ultrasonic Sensors
ESP32 Architecture & Wireless Connectivity
Interactive Joystick Game Logic Development
Fundamentals, Protocols & System Layers of IoT
Hardware-Software-Network Co-Design

6. File Preparation

The vector design was cleaned and prepared before importing it into RDWorks. The outer boundary was prepared as a closed path for through-cutting, while the internal kitten artwork was assigned to engraving:

Step What Was Done
Vector Cleaning Unwanted and overlapping lines were removed.
Scaling The complete design was scaled to fit within a 50 mm × 50 mm acrylic piece.
Path Verification The outer square was checked as a closed path for through-cutting.
Engraving Paths The kitten, heart, paws, facial details, whiskers and "RUBI" text were retained as engraving paths.
Duplicate Removal Duplicate or overlapping geometry was removed to prevent repeated laser passes.
Final Verification The complete design was checked using the RDWorks preview before starting the machine.

7. Layer Setup & Layout in RDWorks

"The design was divided into two operations. The outer square was assigned to the cutting layer, while the complete kitten artwork inside the boundary was assigned to the engraving layer."

Layer Colour Operation Content
Layer 1 Black CUT Outer 50 mm × 50 mm square
Layer 2 Blue ENGRAVE Kitten, heart, paws, facial details, whiskers and "RUBI" text

Material Layout

A 50 mm × 50 mm transparent acrylic piece (2 mm thickness) was used. The design was positioned centrally on the acrylic workpiece. The outer rectangular boundary was assigned to the cutting layer, while the complete kitten artwork inside the boundary was assigned to the engraving layer.

Figure 7.1: RDWorks layout showing the cut (black outer square) and engraving (blue internal artwork) layers.

8. Final Machine Settings

The final laser settings were configured separately for the cutting and engraving operations. The cutting operation was used only for the outer square, and the engraving operation was used for all internal elements of the kitten design:

Material Thickness Operation Speed Min. Power Max. Power Passes Frequency
Acrylic – 2 Transparent 2 mm Cut 18.0 mm/s 55% 65% 1 20 kHz
Acrylic – 2 Transparent 2 mm Engrave 100.0 mm/s 25% 30% 1 20 kHz

9. Cutting and Engraving Process

The prepared DXF file was imported into RDWorks and the cutting and engraving layers were verified. The laser operation was carried out in the following sequence:

1 Position the 50 mm × 50 mm transparent acrylic sheet on the laser bed.
2 Verify the design position in RDWorks.
3 Process the engraving layer to create the kitten and "RUBI" artwork.
4 Cut only the outer square through the acrylic.
5 Maintain air assist and exhaust systems during operation.
6 Remove and clean the final acrylic piece.

Final Appearance

"The transparent acrylic remained as the base workpiece, while the engraved portions produced a frosted, high-contrast translucent appearance because the engraved surface reflects light differently from the original smooth surface."

Complete Digital Fabrication Master Archive (Everything Included)

This master archive brings together the entire Week 06 digital fabrication curriculum, demonstrating both subtractive manufacturing (CO₂ laser cutting and engraving) and additive manufacturing (FDM 3D printing). Both processes successfully transformed digital vector and mesh models into physical tangible artifacts.

Part A • Laser Cutting & Vector Acrylic Fabrication
Subtractive

Converted vector graphics to DXF format, prepared toolpaths in RDWorks V8, calibrated focal waist and air assist, and fabricated the personalized "RUBI" acrylic badge on 2 mm Transparent Acrylic with frosted translucent engraving and clean through-cut perimeter.

Workpiece 50 × 50 mm
Material Acrylic – 2 Transparent
Cut Speed 18 mm/s (55-65%)

Digital Fabrication Comparison: Subtractive vs. Additive

Parameter Laser Cutting (Subtractive) 3D Printing (Additive)
Fabrication Type Subtractive (Material vaporized/cut away by laser beam) Additive (Thermoplastic deposited layer-by-layer)
Machine Used Forge HW Junction DFab #2 (1490 CO₂ Laser) Bambu Lab H2S 3D Printer
Primary Software RDWorks V8 (2D Vector DXF Engine) Bambu Studio (3D Mesh G-code Slicer)
Materials Used Acrylic – 2 Transparent (2 mm thickness) 1.75 mm PLA Filament
Dimensional Scope 50 mm × 50 mm 2D profile + surface raster engrave 48.60 mm height complex volumetric 3D organic geometry
Production Speed High speed (under 3 minutes) 1 hour 9 minutes (243 sequential layers)
Post-Processing Wiping debris and cleaning surface with cloth Carefully removing organic tree support structures
